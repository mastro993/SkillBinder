# Application shell (continued)

The app starts on Library. Back and Forward walk an in-memory history of routes: Explore, Library, a library skill's page (`skill/{id}`), a workspace's page (`workspace/{id}`), an agent's page (`agent/{id}`, by registry id), Universal's page (`agents/universal`), Sync, and Settings. They are also available from the Go menu and with Cmd+[ and Cmd+] on macOS, or Ctrl+[ and Ctrl+] on Windows and Linux. Opening a new route after going back discards the forward routes. A route whose skill no longer exists opens Library with every skill, including while a removed skill's page is shown. The page of a removed workspace, the page of an agent that is not one of your agents (not chosen, or no longer detected), and Universal's page once no universal agent is yours open Library, including while they are shown (ADR 0012). A universal agent's page opens Universal's. History is not kept across restarts, and it is unavailable during onboarding. Settings lists your agents with a switch each above Application, and app updates below it; agents found later and a newer SkillBinder each get a notice at the window's bottom-right ([Settings › Agents](settings-agents.md)).

Users can resize it by dragging its edge or with the arrow, Home, and End keys on its resize control. Width is clamped to 192 through 400 logical pixels. It can be hidden from the sidebar toggle, the View menu, or Cmd+B on macOS and Ctrl+B on Windows and Linux. A hidden sidebar is removed entirely, with no icon rail. Width and hidden state are machine-local preferences that persist across restarts. Preferences stored before the hidden state existed load as visible.

The sidebar toggle stays at one position whether the sidebar is open or closed. On macOS it sits just right of the traffic lights, and elsewhere it sits at the leading edge of the header row. While the sidebar is hidden, Back and Forward leave its header row and lead the page header, 4 points after the toggle and 4 points apart, so the window controls read as one group. Opening and closing take 220 ms with cubic ease-in-out. The sidebar's footprint widens or narrows, so the main area reflows. Meanwhile the full-width sidebar stays pinned to the moving edge and slides in or out. The header's leading inset follows the same curve to stay clear of the toggle. A closing sidebar unmounts after its transition so its controls leave keyboard order. The launch state appears without motion. When the app reports reduced motion, the change happens immediately.

The window is two flat columns, after the GitHub Copilot desktop app (`DESIGN.md` § Layout): the sidebar on its tinted canvas and the page column on the page color, split by a 1-point `border` line on the sidebar's trailing edge, with no gap, panel, or shadow between them. The page header starts at the sidebar's right edge, is 52 points tall, sits on the page color, and has a 1-point `border` line under it; onboarding's has no line. On macOS the traffic lights sit 16 points from the left edge, vertically centred in that row, and the toggle follows one light-spacing after them. Its leading breadcrumb names the current page under its sidebar section, aligned with the page content's 16-point gutter: Explore, Library, Library › the skill's slug, Sync, and Settings. Only Library before a skill name is a link, opening Library. While Library lists every skill, the folder tabs take the breadcrumb's place (see Library and organization), a workspace's page draws its own head there: its glyph, name, a dot, and folder ([Workspace activation](workspace-activation.md)), and an agent's page and Universal's draw theirs: the logo, name, the stack of agents sharing the directory, and the directory ([Workspaces and scanning](workspaces-and-scanning.md#agent-pages)). Pages carry no title of their own. A long title truncates with an ellipsis rather than pushing out its trailing group, which holds buttons that the active screen supplies, inset 16 points so they line up with the page's content. On macOS the header row doubles as the window drag region across the whole window width, over the sidebar as well as the page; double-clicking it performs the system title bar action. A click, double-click, or drag on a folder tab is the tab's own and never performs the title bar action or moves the window. Onboarding shows the header without the toggle, breadcrumb, folder tabs, or screen buttons. Below the header every page fills the rest of the page column. The page scrolls on its own inside the column, which clips it, and opening another route, including a fallback after a refresh removes the shown skill, workspace, or agent, starts it at the top. Every page's content keeps a 16-point gutter from the column's edges: Explore, Library, Settings, a workspace's page, an agent's page, and Universal's lay out their own column with it, and the shell gives every other page the same gutter. Every page's scroll area spans the column with the gutter inside it, so the GPUI Kit scrollbar shows at the column's trailing edge, never over the content, while the pointer is over the page or drags its thumb, fading in and out exactly like the sidebar's. Opening another route mid-drag ends the drag. Content continuing past the top or bottom of the scroll area fades into the page color over 16 points.

The appearance toggle shows the current theme, a sun for light and a moon for dark, and switches to the other one. Its tooltip and accessibility label name the theme it switches to. The toggle has two visible states but stores one of three preferences: System, Light, or Dark. A press that switches away from the system appearance stores that theme as an override. A press that switches back to the system appearance clears the override, so the app follows the system again. The override is checked only when the toggle is pressed. If the system appearance later changes to match an override, the override is kept, so the app stays on that theme when the system changes again. The preference is machine-local and persists across restarts. Preferences stored before the choice existed follow the system appearance. Settings › General › Theme picks any of the three directly ([Settings](settings-agents.md#settings-page)), and the toggle follows it.

Every clickable control shows a hand cursor while enabled and the arrow while disabled: buttons, including the sidebar toggle and dialog close buttons; checkboxes; segments of segmented controls; breadcrumb links; Library's placement areas, pills, switch, and Updates chip; the workspace page's agents buttons and agent avatars; and warning glyphs for copies that are not applied. GPUI Kit buttons keep the arrow except link and text variants, so the design system's `Button` adds the hand cursor, and dialogs replace the kit's close control with its close preset in the title row. That button is hidden while an operation is pending, as the kit control was. The one exception is the close button of the toast that confirms a change: it is Kit's notification's own, which the app cannot restyle, so it keeps the arrow.
